One of the most common worries before a hair transplant is the recovery. The good news? For most patients, it is far gentler than they expect. Here is a realistic, month-by-month timeline so you know exactly what is normal.

## Days 1–3: The immediate phase

Expect some **swelling** — usually around the forehead — and mild tenderness. Small crusts form around each graft. Leave them alone; they are your grafts' protective scabs.

- Sleep with your head slightly elevated.
- Use the provided gentle cleansing spray.
- **Do not** rub, scratch, or shower directly on the recipient area.

## Days 4–7: Early healing

Swelling subsides and crusts begin to loosen. You can usually wash your hair with a gentle technique from day 4–5. The donor area will feel a little sensitive but heals quickly.

## Weeks 2–3: The "shedding" phase

Around week 2–3, transplanted hairs **fall out**. Do not panic — this is completely normal. The follicle itself stays in the scalp and is entering its resting phase. The implantation signs become invisible and you can return fully to normal styling.

## Months 1–3: The quiet phase

Little visibly changes, which tests many patients' patience. Beneath the surface, the grafted follicles are building the blood supply they need for new growth.

## Months 3–5: New growth appears

Fine new hairs begin to push through. This is the first visible reward and usually the moment clients start feeling optimistic.

## Months 5–8: Thickening

Growth accelerates and becomes more noticeable. Expect some variation in texture and direction now — this settles over time.

## Months 9–12: Established results

The majority of patients see a significant, natural-looking improvement. Density continues to mature.

## Months 12–18: Final result

The definitive, permanent outcome. Hair has reached its final thickness, texture, and curl. This is the result you will keep.

> **Pro tip:** document your progress monthly with the same lighting and angle. It is the best way to appreciate how far you have come.

## How to speed up recovery

- Follow your personalised aftercare plan exactly.
- Avoid direct sun and heavy sweating for the first two weeks.
- Stay out of strong heat (saunas, steam rooms) for a month.
- Eat well, hydrate, and avoid smoking — it directly reduces scalp blood flow.

Every patient is different, so treat this as a guide, not a promise. Your clinic will tailor your timeline during follow-up reviews.
