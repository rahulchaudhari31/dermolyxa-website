If you are researching hair restoration, you have almost certainly come across two acronyms: **FUE** and **DHI**. Both are revolutionary advances over the older strip method, but they are not identical. Understanding the difference helps you choose the technique — and the surgeon — that is right for you.

## What is FUE?

Follicular Unit Extraction (FUE) is a method of harvesting individual follicular units using micro-punches as small as 0.7 mm. There is no linear scar, healing is rapid, and each graft is placed under magnification.

The key detail about FUE: the **implantation phase is typically performed by a separate team** after extraction. The surgeon creates tiny recipient sites first, and technicians then place grafts into those pre-made sites.

## What is DHI?

Direct Hair Implantation (DHI) uses a dedicated tool called a **Choi implanter pen**. Grafts are loaded into the pen and planted directly into the donor-matched channel — no pre-made slits are needed.

> In simple terms: FUE extracts like DHI, but DHI implants directly and simultaneously using a single-purpose device.

## The key differences

- **Technique**: FUE separates extraction from implantation; DHI combines them into one continuous process.
- **Control**: DHI allows your surgeon to control depth, angle, and direction of every single graft in real time.
- **Speed**: FUE can be faster over very large sessions; DHI is more precise but can take longer.
- **Density**: DHI can achieve very high density per session thanks to its precision placement.
- **Suitability**: DHI is excellent for hairlines and eyebrow restoration; FUE excels at large-scale crown work.
- **Cost**: DHI is usually priced higher due to the specialist equipment and skill involved.

## Which one is better?

There is no universal winner. A skilled surgeon can deliver outstanding results with either technique. In practice, many clinics — including our own — use **a hybrid approach**: extract with FUE, then implant key zones such as the hairline with DHI-style precision.

What matters far more than the acronym is:

1. The experience and consistency of your surgeon.
2. The quality and density matching of the donor-region plan.
3. Realistic expectations set during consultation.

## Our recommendation

Book a consultation and ask to see both approaches explained on **your own** scalp anatomy. A great clinic will be transparent about technique, graft counts, and honest about what is achievable.

**Summary:** choose FUE for large-scale, cost-conscious restoration and DHI for maximum control and precision in smaller, high-visibility zones.
