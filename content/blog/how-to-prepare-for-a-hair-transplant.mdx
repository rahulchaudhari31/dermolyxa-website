A hair transplant is a procedure, not an event. How you prepare in the weeks before the big day can genuinely influence graft survival and final density. This checklist covers everything we ask every patient to do.

## Four weeks before

- **Share your medical history** in full — including all supplements, medication, and vitamins. Some products (like biotin-heavy supplements) matter more than you think.
- **Quit smoking.** Nicotine constricts blood vessels and reduces the oxygen supply to healing grafts. Most surgeons recommend stopping at least two weeks before.
- **Reduce alcohol** to support healthy circulation and clotting.
- **Stabilise your hair loss** with your physician. If you plan to use medical therapy such as finasteride or minoxidil, it should be part of a settled routine well before surgery.

## Two weeks before

- **Stop blood-thinning agents** — including aspirin, ibuprofen, and fish oil — but only after clearing this with your doctor.
- **Avoid excessive sun.** Serious sunburn on the scalp or donor area can compromise a session.
- **Confirm travel and accommodation** if your clinic is away from home, including the day of procedure and first follow-up.

## The week before

- Arrange **time off** — plan for at least 4–7 days before returning to public-facing work.
- Stock up on the **aftercare products** your clinic lists.
- Wash your hair thoroughly in the 1–2 days before; clean skin is easier for your surgeon.

## The night before

- Sleep well and eat a balanced evening meal.
- Wash your hair as instructed (usually the night before and morning of).
- Wear a **button-up or zip-up shirt** — you will not want to pull anything over your head afterwards.

## On the day

- Arrive with a **full stomach** but keep it light.
- Bring a loose cap, sunglasses, and a soft pillow.
- Leave your phone on silent; the day is focused purely on you.

> **Remember:** the single biggest factor in your result is choosing an experienced, honest team — and then listening to them.

## After your procedure

Follow the recovery timeline in our journal, keep every review appointment, and trust the process. Hair restoration is a marathon, not a sprint — and the finish line is more than worth it.
